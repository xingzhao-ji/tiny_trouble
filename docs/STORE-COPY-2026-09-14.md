# Tiny Trouble — store and launch draft

Current product: **Breakfast Preview**, version 0.2.7. Three connected chapter stages plus the original standalone Toast Express demo. The proposed 60-stage campaign is unfinished. These materials support a private playtest or free preview; they do not justify selling an advertised complete campaign.

## Short description

Meet Pip, a tiny creature with a very useful Scale Spool. Shrink a sugar cube, turn a teaspoon into a bridge, balance blueberries and sail a cereal raft across a milk harbor. Small changes open enormous possibilities in this solo tabletop puzzle adventure.

## About this preview

The breakfast table is a whole world when you're Pip-sized. Change the size of familiar objects and use their new shapes and weight to find a way home.

- **Crumb Crossing:** clear a sugar-blocked passage, carry a teaspoon through a narrow gap, and grow it into a bridge.
- **Blueberry Ballast:** place and resize berries to solve a little balancing puzzle.
- **Milk Harbor:** grow a cereal raft, lighten its cargo and unfold a spoon dock on the far shore.
- **The original Toast Express:** a separate demonstration of the adventure, ending with an unlikely ride home.

Return to Pip's matchbox Workshop to continue saved adventures or replay cleared stages. Adjust music, effects, text size, reduced motion and keyboard controls before playing. Local saves keep progress between visits. No account, AI subscription or internet service is needed for gameplay.

The preview includes three of the five planned Breakfast chapter stages. Later worlds are in development planning. Keyboard/mouse behavior is tested with generated native input on this Mac; controller and physical mobile-device behavior remain unverified.

Suggested tags: Puzzle, 3D, Cozy, Singleplayer, Cute, Exploration. Treat these as positioning proposals, not measured audience demand. Do not promise a playtime until unfamiliar players establish it.

## Pricing and profitability work

**Near-term proposal: a free three-stage preview.** It allows the completed mechanics to earn player trust without charging for planned content. A paid Breakfast release would require the two remaining stages, human presentation acceptance, reliable Windows execution and a credible observed playtime. Only after those conditions hold, compare a one-time $4.99/$7.99/$9.99 price against actual player value and production/support costs. These are hypotheses, not a commitment or revenue forecast.

Use the [local economics worksheet](../../output/release-preparation-2026-09-14/economics.html). Enter actual development hours, cash costs, price, refunds, regional discounts, store terms and expected orders. Default values are examples; the store-share field is an assumption to replace with the signed agreement. Break-even is a cost calculation, not evidence that buyers exist.

Avoid ongoing paid infrastructure: this game runs locally and contains no LLM feature that requires replacing a cloud API. No account, backend, ads, subscription or in-app purchase is needed for this proposal.

## Validation and launch sequence

1. Review the exact 0.2.7 Mac/Windows candidate, store screenshots and ordinary-size gameplay. Keep human audio/presentation judgment separate from automated checks.
2. Observe 12–20 unfamiliar cozy-puzzle players with the existing neutral protocol. Record where they get stuck, which size changes they discover, voluntary continuation and their reasons. Keep individual observations, not invented satisfaction percentages.
3. Before adding more content, address repeated usability/presentation problems from that review. Record target price and observed playtime without leading participants toward a preferred answer.
4. Complete the intended paid scope and verify Windows on named hardware; test physical controls, saves, installation and repeated sessions. Keep the preview clearly labeled until then.
5. Use the prepared SteamPipe staging after actual account/app/depot setup and review. No external invitations, uploads, payments or store changes have been made.

Steam Direct currently charges $100 per app, recoupable after $1,000 adjusted gross revenue. Account eligibility and exact terms must be checked before paying.
